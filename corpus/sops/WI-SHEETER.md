## 1. Purpose
Response to sheeter faults on flatbread lines, including gearbox seizure.

## 2. Immediate actions
2.1 Stop the line, isolate, and apply lock-out tag-out before any inspection.

2.2 Notify the shift supervisor with a preliminary time-to-restore estimate
within 10 minutes. The supervisor cannot plan product recovery without an
estimate, and an uncertain estimate given early is more useful than a precise
one given late.

2.3 Revise the estimate as soon as the fault is characterised. Understating
time-to-restore causes staged dough to be held rather than transferred, and
it then expires.

## 3. Fault categories and typical durations
3.1 Belt tracking misalignment -- 15 to 30 minutes.

3.2 Roller bearing noise without seizure -- 30 to 60 minutes.

3.3 **Gearbox seizure** -- 90 to 180 minutes. Requires drive decoupling and
in most cases a gearbox exchange from spares. If the spare gearbox is not on
site, escalate immediately; lead time from the regional store is 4 to 6
hours.

3.4 Drive motor failure -- 120 to 240 minutes.

## 4. Gearbox exchange
4.1 Decouple the drive, support the gearbox, and remove per the OEM manual.

4.2 Inspect the input shaft and coupling for damage. A seized gearbox
frequently damages the coupling, and replacing the gearbox alone results in a
repeat failure within days.

4.3 Fill to the specified level with food-grade lubricant only. Use of
non-food-grade lubricant on a product-contact machine is a critical
non-conformance and requires product hold.

## 5. Clearance for restart
5.1 Confirm guard interlocks reinstated and functional.

5.2 Run the sheeter empty for 5 minutes and confirm no abnormal noise,
temperature, or vibration.

5.3 Inspect for tooling, fasteners, or rags left in the machine. Record the
tool count out and back in.

5.4 Issue written clearance to the shift supervisor. The line then restarts
under SOP-RESTART-FLAT section 4.

## 6. Records
Maintenance work order, parts used, and the tool count record. Retained 36
months.
