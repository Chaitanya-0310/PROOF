## 1. Purpose
To define when and by whom a customer is told that a shipment will be short
or late.

## 2. Principle
2.1 The customer is told as soon as the shortfall is known with reasonable
confidence, not when it is certain and not when the truck is loading.

2.2 A customer who is told early can re-plan. A customer who discovers a
shortfall at receipt cannot, and the commercial cost of the second case is
materially higher.

## 3. Timing by customer tier
3.1 **Strategic tier:** notify within 60 minutes of the shortfall being
identified, and before any recovery action is executed that changes the
delivery profile.

3.2 **Core tier:** notify within 4 hours, or by 16:00 local on the day
preceding the ship date, whichever is earlier.

3.3 **Standard tier:** notify by end of the business day on which the
shortfall is identified.

## 4. Who notifies
4.1 The commercial account owner makes the contact. Plant staff do not
contact customers directly.

4.2 The plant supplies the account owner with the expected shortfall
quantity, the revised availability time, and the cause in one message.
Partial information forces a second call and erodes confidence.

4.3 For strategic-tier accounts, the account owner is notified before a
reallocation is executed, not after. See SOP-LINE-REALLOC section 3.3.

## 5. Content
5.1 State the affected order, the shortfall in cases and units, the revised
availability, and whether a partial shipment is offered.

5.2 Do not speculate on cause beyond what is established. "A mechanical fault
on the production line" is sufficient; naming an unconfirmed root cause
creates commitments that may not survive investigation.

## 6. Escalation
6.1 Shortfalls exceeding 20 percent of an order line, or any shortfall to a
strategic-tier account, are escalated to the commercial director the same
day.

## 7. Records
Notification records are retained 24 months and reviewed in the monthly
service review.
